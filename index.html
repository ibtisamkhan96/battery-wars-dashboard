LFP's Takeover (global share of EV batteries)

Iron-phosphate was written off as "low-end" five years ago. In 2025 it overtook nickel chemistries globally for the first time.

LFP Share by Region, 2025

The chemistry map IS the geopolitics map: China bet on cheap iron, the West bet on nickel — and iron won on cost.

Why It Matters

LFP needs no nickel and no cobalt — the two most contested minerals in the EV supply chain. China controls most LFP cathode production and holds the patents, the process know-how, and the cost curve. That single chemistry decision, made a decade ago, is a big part of why Chinese cells are ~20-30% cheaper per kWh and why Tesla, Ford and VW now buy or license Chinese LFP technology for their entry models.

The Solid-State Race

The next battlefield: solid electrolytes promise ~2x energy density and 10-minute charging. Timelines below are company targets — treat with healthy skepticism.
2026
🇺🇸 QuantumScape (VW)

First qualification samples of lithium-metal solid-state cells; industrialization deal with VW's PowerCo. Vehicle integration targeted ~2028.

2026–27
🇯🇵 Toyota

Limited production start in Japan; targets 1,000 km range and 10-minute charging with sulfide electrolytes. Mass production "2030 and beyond."

2027
🇰🇷 Samsung SDI

Pilot lines running; oxide-based cells aimed at premium EVs first — Korea's best shot at regaining ground.

2027–28
🇨🇳 CATL & BYD

Semi-solid-state cells already shipping in limited models; both hedge with sodium-ion for cheap segments. Scale advantage may decide this race too.
